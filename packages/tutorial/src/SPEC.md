# The tutorial: spec (`packages/tutorial/main.js`, built from `packages/tutorial/src/`)

A new story for the app in `/home/user/airportnetworks` (read `AGENTS.md` once for context and conventions, `CLAUDE.md`
for the working rules). A robot guide stands in your room and shows you how the app works in a headset: pointing,
playing a story, explore mode, the menu, Recenter, shared rooms. About three and a half minutes of story (187 s of story time, at least 3:26 recorded), then explore
mode is **practice**: seven small tasks the guide reacts to, and the small network from its chest to point at.

The guide is the street-armour mannequin of people's bodies (`bodies.js`, posed by `ik.js`), driven not by a person
but by code: its head and two hands follow scripted poses that are pure functions of story time (so seeking works),
and `ik.js` does arms, legs and footsteps. It is a robot and says so. Parts of its body become the props: the chest
emblem flies off and grows into a network, the five spine nodes on its back become five chapter markers on the floor,
it copies itself in another colour to explain shared rooms, and it powers down at the end.

The owner's decisions (November 2026): only the robot (no robot vacuums); AR and VR first, the page done differently
later (for now it must load and run, with a notice); its moves and words are written by Claude and the credits say so;
the stories' synthetic voice (Kokoro `af_heart`, `tools/voice/narrate.mjs`); British English in the text; sound only
through `HOST.audio.fx` with existing `SFX_LIB` sounds, no hum or background.

Everything below is a plan: numbers are starting values to be tuned in the harness and on a headset.

## B. The shell change: story-driven figures

Today `HOST.bodies` has one draw set, begun and ended only by `shared.js`'s `drawOverlay`, only in an XR session, into
the overlay's root. A story must draw its own figures, on a page too, without touching people's bodies.

### `bodies.js`

**Draw sets.** The per-draw state (`body`, `halo`, `tex`, `data`, `inst`, `col`, `set`, `uni`, `n`, `calls`) moves
out of `W` into a set made by `newSet(cap)`. `make()` keeps what is shared: the mannequin, its two base geometries,
the shader sources, the calibration. A set has its own `DataTexture` (`70 x cap` texels: 69 as today plus `aSet`), its own instance attributes,
its own two `ShaderMaterial`s (same sources, so three.js links the programs once) and two `InstancedBufferGeometry`
wrappers that share the base attributes.

- The existing API (`begin`, `add`, `end`, `attach`, `stats`, `meshes`, `CAP` 49) is the default set's, unchanged:
  `shared.js` keeps using it as it is.
- New: `HOST.bodies.set(cap)` returns `S = { attach(parent), begin(t), add(B, I), end(), dispose(), stats(), meshes() }`,
  cap 1 to 8 (the tutorial: 2). `begin(t)` sets that set's own `uTime` (the tutorial passes story time in ms, so the
  scan halts when the story pauses). `dispose()` removes its two meshes from their parent and disposes its texture and
  materials, **never** the geometries (their attributes are shared with every other set; a story's `dispose()` that
  traverses `GL.scene` must call `S.dispose()` first).
- `person(id)` is independent of sets: one figure can be drawn by any set.

**Parts.** Every vertex and halo vertex gets a part id, from an optional `part` on `put`, `spot`,
`glow` and the halo items, inherited by everything made inside a `part` block; the weld key (`piece()`, ~l.415) gains
the part so welded vertices never mix parts. Triangle counts are unchanged. The id is **packed into an existing float**,
not a new attribute: `aMat + 4 * part` in `VERT` and `aKind + 4 * part` in `HVERT` (read back with `floor(x / 4.0)` and
`mod(x, 4.0)`; check in `make()` that both hold only 0 to 3 today, else use a larger stride). With `aSet` in the data
texture, neither shader gains an attribute: `VERT` stays at 7 and `HVERT` at 8, within WebGL1's guaranteed 8.

| Id | Part | Made at (bodies.js, about) | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | the body: plates, undersuit, joint balls, the badge plate under the emblem | everything else | hide all |
| 1 | the chest emblem: four hexes, six links, their spots | `emblem()` ~l.232-242 (the call ~l.303) | hidden when it flies off |
| 2 | the five spine nodes and their spots | ~l.310-313 | hidden when they fly to the floor |
| 3 | the visor's light | ~l.254-255 | boot-up, power-down |
| 4 | helmet lights: brows, crest, band, ear pods | ~l.256-272 | boot-up |
| 5 | torso lights: collar, chest V, chest traces, the spine strip, the belt strips | ~l.279-327 | boot-up |
| 6 | arm lights: shoulder rims, elbow gems, bracers and their traces, knuckles | ~l.343-358 | boot-up; the "signal down the arm" |
| 7 | leg lights: thigh lights and traces, knee rings, calf strips, shin ridges, boot cuffs | ~l.367-383 | boot-up |
| 8 | boots: the soles' side lights and the floor pools | ~l.390-393 | boot-up, the glide's trail |

A light painted onto a plate (`glow(...)` on a piece) puts that whole piece in the part: harmless, because only parts
1 and 2 (separate pieces) are ever hidden; the others are only darkened.

**Per-figure look.** `add(B, I)` takes four new optional fields, written as one more texel, `aSet` (vec4), at the end of the
figure's row of the set's `DataTexture` (rows grow from 69 to 70 texels; the shaders already fetch that row),
and two that change only the solve (`I.ease`, `I.warm`, below the table):

| Field | Meaning | Default (people) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `I.hide` | bit mask over part ids 0 to 8 (bit `p` = part `p` not drawn; 511 = the whole figure) | 0 |
| `I.dark` | bit mask: those parts' lights off (glow and halo 0; the plate stays) | 0 |
| `I.power` | 0 to 1: every light and halo times it; the rim light and the plates' brightness `mix(0.25, 1, power)` and `mix(0.45, 1, power)` | 1 |
| `I.flash` | `[part, a]`: that part's lights pushed towards white by `0.6 a` (a part lighting up, a signal) | none (`aSet.w = 0`) |

- `I.ease` (seconds, default 0.2): the time constant of the hands' easing, the one line `H.w = dt && !jump ? H.w +
  (want - H.w) * (1 - Math.exp(-dt / 0.2)) : want` (~l.773) reading `I.ease` instead of `0.2`, with `0` meaning "no
  easing: `H.w = want`". People keep 0.2; the guide passes 0, so its hands follow the script exactly (a T-pose snapped
  in 0.15 s, dead still, arms to its sides in 0.12 s) and the script's own eases are not smoothed a second time.
- `I.warm: true`: solve only. The solver's state (feet, hips, velocity, hands) advances, but no row is written, `n`
  is not counted and the cap is never reached. Used to re-warm after a jump (section C, "Seeking and pausing").

`aSet = [hide, dark, power, flash ? part + min(a, 0.99) : 0]`. Part 0 (the body) cannot be flashed (`w = 0` reads as
none); only lights flash, and the body has none of its own. Bits are read without integer maths (WebGL1):
`bit(m, p) = mod(floor(m / exp2(p)), 2.0) > 0.5`. In `VERT` and `HVERT`: a hidden part's vertex goes behind the far
plane (`gl_Position = vec4(0, 0, 2, 1)`, the trick `HVERT` already uses for a folded neck); `vOn = (1 - dark bit) *
power` and `vFl` (the flash amount for this vertex's part) are passed as varyings, so the fragment shader gains only
two multiplies and a mix. The scan keeps working (it is keyed to `uTime`, so a paused tutorial's scan stands still).
With all defaults the shaders give exactly today's pixels: people's bodies do not change (the smoke check below).

**Helpers** (about 25 lines):

- `HOST.bodies.size(B, eye)`: a fixed size: `sizeTo(B, eye)`, `B.fixed = true`, and `eyeStep` skips a fixed figure
  (no sampler, no easing: a crouch bends the legs, never shrinks it). It also sets `B.eye.now` (and the eased value)
to `eye`, since `info()` reads them. The guide: `size(B, 1.62)` (k = 1: 1.75 m tall).
- `HOST.bodies.grip(side, wrist, aim, out)`: the grip pose (`[x, y, z, qx, qy, qz, qw]`) that puts the wrist at
  `wrist` with the forearm along `aim`: `q = fromTo(T-pose arm direction (+x left, -x right), aim) * GRIP_Q[side]`, the
  position the wrist plus `FIST` turned by `q`. Palms stay down for forward aims; a script never builds a quaternion
  for a hand.
- `HOST.bodies.point(B, name, I, out)`: the world place of a named rest point after the last `add`: the bone's
  `matrixWorld` (times `I.toWorld`) applied to the point minus the bone's rest place. Names: `emblem0` (hub) to
  `emblem3`, `spine0` (top) to `spine4`, `visor`, `chest`, `lfist`, `rfist`, `lboot`, `rboot`. The props start from
  these, so a flying emblem leaves exactly from the chest. This needs a table of rest points that `mannequin()` records
as it builds them: `M.pts = { name: [bone, x, y, z] }` (rest place, set in `emblem()`, the spine nodes, the visor, the
chest plate, the fists and the boots); today only `M.at` (the bones' places) exists.
- `info(B)` gains `started` (the foot that began a step this frame, -1 if none), for tests.

## C. The guide's motion system

### Frames and places

- The set's frame: floor at y = 0, 1 unit = 1 m, the viewer's place when the scene was placed at x = z = 0, ahead
  along -z, right +x. In a headset the set hangs under `GL.world`, which hangs under `GL.rig` (level, set from the head:
  `xrPlace`): `GL.world.position = (0, floorY - rigY, 0)` in the rig's frame, where `floorY` is the floor plane's
  height if the session reports planes (the robots' floor rule: the plane labelled floor, the largest; else the lowest
  large upward plane over a metre below the head; read once a second until found, then kept, re-placed only if it moves
  more than 5 cm), else 1.6 m below the head at placing (the flock's rule). Same in VR and AR. Never moves `GL.rig`.
- At a station in a shared room the frame is the station's (the shell's `place` hook), so the guide stands 2 m in front
  of the circle's centre, facing it.
- The body set is attached to `GL.scene` (an identity parent) and `I.toWorld = set.group.matrixWorld` each frame, so
  the bone matrices follow the rig and anchors and the boots' pools land on the true floor (`fy`).

| Place | x, z (m) | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home: where the guide stands to talk | 0.35, -2.00 | 10 degrees right of ahead; the panel hangs 33 degrees left (`xrPanelPose` home -0.56, -0.86) |
| The five markers (an arc of radius 2.4 m) | bearings -10, 2, 14, 26, 38 degrees: (-0.42, -2.36), (0.08, -2.40), (0.58, -2.33), (1.05, -2.16), (1.48, -1.89) | 0.5 m apart, one step each |
| The network's centre (height 1.30 m) | -0.05, -1.25 | radius 0.35 m when grown: its nearest node 0.9 m from the viewer's start |
| The pointing target (height 1.50 m) | -0.60, -1.80 | between the panel and the guide |
| The copy's place | 1.25, -2.10 | |
| The glide (chapter 5) | from home to 1.85, -2.00 and back | |
| End: by the panel | -0.15, -1.70 | the nearest the script comes: 1.71 m |

Rules the script keeps (checked by `test-script.mjs`): the guide's head is never nearer than 1.2 m (in x, z) to the
frame's origin; walks are at most 0.7 m/s at cruise in story time (so `ik.js` steps: it glides above 1.0 m/s) and
glides at least 1.6 m/s at cruise in story time, and no stretch of more than 0.3 s lies between 0.85 and 1.15 m/s.
These are story speeds; `narrate.mjs` slows each stretch by its own factor (at least 1.1), so the same rules are
checked a second time in **real** time, with the final `NARRATION_SYNC` applied (a glide whose real speed drops under
1.3 m/s is lengthened in story time, never slowed); the head never rises above `1.02 * 1.62` m standing.
Because the viewer can walk, a live guard (per device, never in state) keeps the clear zone: with the viewer's head
within 0.6 m of the guide's head its `power` is capped at 0.3 and its halo off; within 0.35 m it is hidden (`hide:
511`; the solver keeps running so it reappears in step). The network and markers are fixed props the viewer may walk
into (their choice), but nothing moves towards the head.

### The timeline (`sets/script.js`)

`SCRIPT` is data written in **story seconds relative to a chapter's start** (`['play', 14.6, ...]`); `buildStory()`
hands the set the chapter starts (`GL.sets.guide.chapters(CH)`), so the timeline follows `CH` and `narrate.mjs`'s
retiming (which stretches the story clock between `NARRATION_SYNC` pairs) moves it with the words. The set has one
keyed param, `guide_clock` (story seconds, keyed linear `0 -> T_END`, in `LIN`), plus `guide_mode` (0 story, 1
practice; a step) and `guide_a` (shown). Everything the guide and its props do is `evalGuide(SCRIPT, clock, live)`:
the same clock gives the same pose; `live` (the viewer's head and rays, the panel's place) only ever turns the head
and visor and aims the end's pointing (per device, like the flock's `see`).

Tracks (each a sorted list of keys; between keys the named ease):

| Track | Keys | Eases |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pos` | `[t, x, z, ease]` the floor point under the head | `walk`: constant speed with 0.3 s of speeding up and slowing down; `glide`: the same at glide speed with 0.1 s ramps (so it crosses the 0.85 to 1.15 band in under 0.1 s); `snap`: 0.15 s |
| `yaw` | `[t, deg, dur]` the heading (0 = facing the viewer's start, i.e. +z towards the origin) | eased in and out over 40% of `dur` at each end (v181, was a constant rate with 60 ms eases), then a 1.5 degree overshoot settling in 0.3 s |
| `face` | `[t, w]` 0 to 1: blends the scripted heading towards "facing the viewer's live head" | linear over 0.4 s |
| `look` | `[t, target]`: `fwd` (pitch 0), a point, `you` (the viewer's head), `ray` (the viewer's ray dot or, with none, the target), `panel:stories`, `panel:sound`, `panel:foot` (the main panel's Stories button, its Sound button (region `sound`, "Narration off" in a headset) and the foot bar: their live places from `XR.panelGroup` and the panel's `P.regions`; on a page fixed points where the panel would hang), `node:i` | head turns at most 80 degrees off the heading; 0.4 s ease between targets (v181, was 0.25 s) |
| life | (no track, v181: `gLife` in `sets/script.js`) never quite still: breathing (eyes and hands 3 to 4 mm at 0.22 Hz), a slow weight shift (head 12 mm side to side with a roll), small drifts of the head (about 2 to 3 degrees) and of the hanging hands; on a walk a side-to-side sway, a 14 mm bob and the arms swinging against the steps, eased in and out with the walk; damped while powered down; sums of slow sines of the clock, so seeking still works; the floor point is never moved | |
| `nod` | `[t, n]` n quick nods: pitch -14 degrees in 0.14 s, back in 0.14 s | |
| `sweep` | `[t]` the visor sweep before it speaks: yaw -15, +15, 0 degrees over 0.6 s | |
| `eye` | `[t, h]` eye height, default 1.61 m; 1.58 for a bow, 1.61 standing | `smooth` 0.3 s |
| `hand.l`, `hand.r` | spans `[t0, t1, gesture, args]`; outside a span the hand takes the `hang` gesture (never `g: null`: with `I.ease = 0` the script does all the easing) | in and out over 0.18 s (or the span's own), ease-out cubic, then dead still |
| `parts` | `[t, 'dark' | 'light' | 'hide' | 'show', partIds]`, `[t, 'flash', part]` (0.35 s decay), `[t, 'power', p, dur]` | steps; power linear |

### Seeking and pausing

The solver keeps state (planted feet, the hips' heading, velocity, hand easing), so a jump in story time (a seek, a
chapter arrow, a late joiner, going backwards, or a gap over 0.5 s) re-warms it: `S.add(B, { ...I, jump: true, warm: true })` at
`clock - 1.0`, then 29 `add`s with `warm: true` at `dt = 1/30`, then the frame's ordinary `add` at `clock` between
`S.begin` and `S.end` (warm steps write no row, so the cap of 2 is never reached; about 1 ms). Paused, `dt = 0`:
the figure is drawn but nothing moves, and `uTime` (story time) stands still, so the scan halts mid-climb. Every device
at a station ends at the same pose (the solver is deterministic given its targets and steps), apart from the head's
live turn towards its own viewer.
