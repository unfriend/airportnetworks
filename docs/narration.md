# Narration scripts

Generated by `node tools/narration.mjs` from the running stories: never edit by hand, run it again after a story's
text changes. For each story, the words on its panel chapter by chapter, with the time each line appears (minutes and
seconds on the recording's clock, from NARRATION_SYNC where the story has one). A narration follows these words and
times; it can say them more freely, but the numbers must stay as they are (they come from the data).

How to record and fit a narration: see "Recording a narration" in `audio/PUT-narration.mp3-HERE.txt`. In short, put the
MP3 at the file named under each story; if your chapters start at other times, change NARRATION_SYNC (pairs of
[time in the recording, story time]) in the story's source (`tools/<story>/engine/e3_story.js`, then rebuild; in
`stories/<story>.js` for the airports, proteins and spatial stories) and run the smoke test.

## Whole-body PET: One scan, one system (`#pet`)

File: `audio/narration-pet.mp3` (not recorded yet). Chapters: 0:00 Intro, 0:16 Scanner, 0:52 Sugar, 1:30 Organs, 2:08 Network, 2:52 Health, 3:38 One scan, 4:18 People, 5:05 Vision; it ends at 5:30.

- **0:02** the body as a metabolic network
- **0:04** From one PET scan to a network of organs: what it says about health, disease and survival.

### 0:16 Scanner

- **0:16** Seeing sugar
- **0:17** A PET scan (positron emission tomography) starts with a sugar. FDG (fluorodeoxyglucose) is a glucose look-alike carrying radioactive fluorine-18.
- **0:20** After at least 6 hours without food, it goes into a vein. For about 60 minutes the person rests: cells take it up like glucose, and an enzyme, hexokinase, traps it inside.
- **0:29** Then the scan. Around the tunnel sits a ring of detectors.
- **0:30** A positron: fluorine-18 decays and sends one out (the electron's antimatter twin); it travels about a millimetre (drawn larger here)
- **0:33** Two photons: it meets an electron and both vanish: two gamma photons of 511 keV (kiloelectronvolts) fly off in opposite directions
- **0:37** A coincidence: two detectors on opposite sides of the ring register both within a few billionths of a second; the line between them, the line of response, passes close to where the sugar is
- **0:43** Millions of such lines make the image: a map of where the body takes up sugar.
- **0:47** The uptake is measured as the SUV, the standardised uptake value: the tracer in a tissue divided by the dose per kilogram of body weight (1 would be an even spread). Protocol as in the paper: 250 to 300 MBq (megabecquerels) of FDG.

### 0:52 Sugar

- **0:52** Where the sugar goes
- **0:53** The tracer arrives with the blood, and within the hour it settles where cells use sugar.
- **1:02** The brain takes up the most, and the heart often does too (how much varies from person to person). The kidneys and the bladder glow too: FDG is filtered into the urine and not taken back, so it leaves the body that way.
- **1:12** A tumour shows as a hot spot. Such spots are mostly what a scan is read for.
- **1:21** Yet the scan holds every organ's metabolism at once.

### 1:30 Organs

- **1:30** Every organ, measured

- **1:44** Each organ's uptake is divided by that of the aorta: the blood pool, the same reference in every scan.
- **1:53** Brain: mean of the whole brain: it lives on sugar
- **1:57** Liver: mean of the whole liver
- **2:01** Muscle: mean of the gluteus maximus (the buttock muscle)
- **2:05** One scan, one row of numbers.
- **2:06** The organs' values shown here are typical uptakes, for illustration.

### 2:08 Network

- **2:08** 658 scans
- **2:09** The paper used 658 scans of 548 people: 241 of healthy volunteers and 417 of people with cancer (165 lung, 252 head and neck).
- **2:15** 658: scans
- **2:16** 11: organs in the network
- **2:18** 7,238: values at most

### 2:52 Health

- **2:52** From health to disease
- **2:53** Split the scans into groups, and give each group its own network. Healthy people's is dense: 30% of the most possible, against 7% with cancer.

## Collective motion: Flock (`#flock`)

File: `audio/narration-flock.mp3` (not recorded yet). Chapters: 0:00 Intro, 0:18 Seven, 0:40 Wave, 1:02 Around you; it ends at 1:12.

### 0:00 Intro

- **0:01** A flock
- **0:02** of starlings at dusk
- **0:04** Before they settle for the night, starlings gather in flocks of hundreds or thousands and sweep across the sky together. These 200 fly all around you.
- **0:10** In a headset, look around: they never leave. On a screen the view follows them.

### 0:18 Seven

- **0:18** Seven neighbours
- **0:19** No bird leads. Each one keeps track of about seven of its nearest neighbours, however near or far they are, and matches their heading and speed.
- **0:25** Draw each bird's link to the seven it follows, and the flock is a network: every bird a node, every link one bird watching another.
- **0:32** The links change from moment to moment, as birds change places.
- **0:34** Ballerini et al., PNAS 2008: starlings follow a set number of neighbours, not all birds within a set distance.

### 0:40 Wave

- **0:40** A wave
- **0:41** A falcon dives in. The birds nearest it turn away, their neighbours follow them, and theirs: a wave of turning runs through the whole flock.
- **0:49** The dark bands you see are birds banking together, their wings edge on or full on to you.
- **0:54** Because every bird is linked to its neighbours, a turn can spread across the whole flock, however big it is.
- **0:56** Cavagna et al., PNAS 2010; Procaccini et al., Animal Behaviour 2011. The flock here is a simulation.

### 1:02 Around you

- **1:02** All around you
- **1:03** The flock flies on for as long as you stay.
- **1:05** Change the number of birds, show who follows whom, or send a falcon: click the sky, or point and pull the trigger in a headset.
