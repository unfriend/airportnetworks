The story is already timed to your recording (chapters at 0:00, 0:16, 0:38,
1:01, 1:17, 1:44, 2:17, 2:46, 2:59 and 3:38, ending at 3:51). If the file runs
longer, the story waits for it to finish.

If you re-record with different timings, change NARRATION_SYNC near the top of
stories/airports.js (pairs of [time in recording, original story cue]).
Without the file the story plays silently on the same timing.

Recording a narration
The airports story has a human recording (narration.mp3); every other story a
synthetic one. A synthetic voice for a new story: see tools/voice/README.md
(node tools/voice/narrate.mjs <key> does steps 1 to 3).
1. Record from docs/narration.md: the words on each story's panel, chapter by
   chapter, with the time each line appears. It is made by
   node tools/narration.mjs http://127.0.0.1:8123/ (serve the folder first);
   run it again after a story's text changes. Keep the numbers as written:
   they come from the data.
2. Save the recording in this folder as the MP3 named above for its story.
3. If your chapters start at other times, change NARRATION_SYNC where named
   above (pairs of [time in the recording, story time]); for the built
   stories, rebuild with node tools/<story>/build.mjs. A recorded story's
   table follows the recording: tools/pace.mjs leaves it alone from then on.
4. Bump VERSION in sw.js. A device keeps a narration once a story has played
   it there; sw.js lists the narration files in AUDIO (all sixteen names are
   there already, and the pass story's German one) so that a new release
   checks the ones a device holds and fetches those that changed.
5. Serve the folder and run node tools/smoke.mjs and node tools/audio.mjs
   (both take the address, e.g. http://127.0.0.1:8123/). Whether it is heard
   in a headset can only be checked there (Sound check on the Stories screen).
